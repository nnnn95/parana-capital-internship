## 1. Strategy Overview

### Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA)
Invest a fixed amount of $500 on the last trading day of every month, regardless of market conditions. Shares are never sold. The strategy benefits from buying more shares when prices are low and fewer when prices are high, naturally reducing the average cost per share over time.

### Momentum Investing
Invest $500 only when the 6-month cumulative return (excluding the most recent month) is positive, indicating an uptrend. When the signal is negative, the $500 is held as cash. This strategy aims to avoid investing during prolonged downtrends.

> The most recent month is skipped in the momentum calculation to reduce short-term reversal noise.

## 3. Key Findings

**DCA outperformed Momentum in absolute returns.**  
DCA generated a final portfolio value of $155,197 versus $130,155 for Momentum — a difference of $25,042. Over 10 years, consistently staying invested proved more profitable than timing the market with a momentum signal.

**Momentum offered marginally lower risk.**  
Maximum drawdown for Momentum (-26.29%) was 3.34 percentage points shallower than DCA (-29.63%), and annualised volatility was slightly lower (40.17% vs 42.50%). This reflects the strategy's ability to preserve cash during sustained downtrends (e.g. the 2022 rate-hike selloff).

**Sharpe ratios are nearly identical.**  
Both strategies delivered equivalent risk-adjusted returns (DCA: 1.50, Momentum: 1.52), meaning the extra return from DCA came with proportionally more risk. Neither strategy dominates the other on a risk-adjusted basis.

## 5. Conclusion

For a passive long-term investor, **DCA is the stronger choice** over this 10-year window: it requires zero market timing, delivers higher absolute wealth, and achieves comparable risk-adjusted returns. The Momentum strategy offers a modest reduction in peak drawdown but at the cost of $25,000 in forgone gains — largely because cash held during negative-signal months missed the sharp recovery periods that followed.

Both strategies significantly outperformed a simple "invest everything on day one" lump-sum approach adjusted for the monthly contribution constraint, confirming that systematic monthly investing in QQQ was a highly effective strategy over the 2014–2023 decade.
